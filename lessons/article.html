هَذَا وَهَذِهِ

This (masculine) & this (feminine) — read it together, tap the pictures to hear them.

هَيَّا نَقْرَأْ مَعًا!

مَاذَا سَنَتَعَلَّمُ؟ What will we learn?

بَعْدَ هَذَا الدَّرْسِ سَتَقُولُ «هَذَا» وَ«هَذِهِ» مِثْلَ الْكِبَارِ! After this lesson you’ll use هَذَا and هَذِهِ like a grown-up!

  • أَقُولُ هَذَا لِلْمُذَكَّرِ.I say هَذَا (“this”) for masculine words.
  • أَقُولُ هَذِهِ لِلْمُؤَنَّثِ.I say هَذِهِ (“this”) for feminine words.
  • أَعْرِفُ التَّاءَ الْمَرْبُوطَةَ: ةI know the tā’ marbūṭa ة and how it makes words feminine.
  • أَعْرِفُ كَلِمَاتٍ مُؤَنَّثَةً بِلَا ة.I know some feminine words that have no ة.
  • أَكْتُبُ هَذَا وَهَذِهِ بِيَدِي.I can write هَذَا and هَذِهِ myself.

١ هَذَا … لِلْمُذَكَّرِ هَذَا — “this” for masculine words

نَقُولُ هَذَا لِلْمُذَكَّرِ. اضْغَطْ عَلَى الصُّورَةِ وَاسْتَمِعْ.

We say hādhā for masculine nouns. Tap a picture to hear it.

سِرٌّ صَغِيرٌ A little secret

أَكْثَرُ الْكَلِمَاتِ الْمُذَكَّرَةِ لَيْسَ فِي آخِرِهَا ة.

Most masculine words do not end in ة.

٢ هَذِهِ … لِلْمُؤَنَّثِ هَذِهِ — “this” for feminine words

نَقُولُ هَذِهِ لِلْمُؤَنَّثِ. انْظُرْ إِلَى آخِرِ الْكَلِمَةِ!

We say hādhihi for feminine nouns. Look at the end of each word — see the glowing letter?

ة

التَّاءُ الْمَرْبُوطَةُ tā’ marbūṭa — the “tied t”

هَذَا الْحَرْفُ يَأْتِي فِي آخِرِ الْكَلِمَةِ، وَيَجْعَلُهَا مُؤَنَّثَةً فِي الْغَالِبِ.

This letter comes at the end of a word, and it usually makes the word feminine.

٣ سِحْرُ التَّاءِ The magic of ة — add it and the word becomes feminine

أَضِفِ التَّاءَ الْمَرْبُوطَةَ… فَتَصِيرُ الْكَلِمَةُ مُؤَنَّثَةً!

Tap + ة and watch the ة fly in. The picture changes too, and هَذَا becomes هَذِهِ.

هَذَا مُعَلِّمٌ

a teacher (man)

هَذَا طَالِبٌ

a student (boy)

هَذَا قِطٌّ

a cat (he)

انْتَبِهْ! Careful! Some feminine words have no ة

مُؤَنَّثٌ بِلَا ة Feminine — but no ة

بَعْضُ الْكَلِمَاتِ مُؤَنَّثَةٌ، وَلَيْسَ فِي آخِرِهَا ة. نَقُولُ مَعَهَا: هَذِهِ.

A few words are feminine even though they don’t end in ة. We still say هَذِهِ with them — we learn these by heart.

تَحَدِّي الْأَبْطَالِ Champions’ challenge — describing words agree

الصِّفَةُ تَتْبَعُ الِاسْمَ: إِذَا كَانَ مُؤَنَّثًا، أَخَذَتِ الصِّفَةُ ة.

A describing word follows its noun: if the noun is feminine, the describing word takes ة too.

انْظُرْ جَيِّدًا Look closely

«بِنْتٌ» بِلَا ة، وَلَكِنَّ صِفَتَهَا فِيهَا ة: بِنْتٌ نَشِيطَةٌ.

بِنْت has no ة, but her describing word still gets one: نَشِيطَة.

مَحَطَّةُ الْكِتَابَةِ Writing station — finger, Apple Pencil or mouse

خُذْ قَلَمَكَ أَوْ إِصْبَعَكَ، وَابْدَأْ مِنَ النُّقْطَةِ الْخَضْرَاءِ.

Arabic is written from right to left — start at the green dot. There is no wrong answer here; tap «انْتَهَيْتُ» when you’re done.

Parents’ corner رُكْنُ الْأَهْلِ

What your child learned today

Arabic nouns are either masculine or feminine, and the word for “this” changes to match: هَذَا (hādhā) for masculine nouns and هَذِهِ (hādhihi) for feminine ones. Most feminine nouns end in the tā’ marbūṭa ة, which turns many masculine words feminine (مُعَلِّمٌ ← مُعَلِّمَةٌ, teacher). A few common feminine words have no ة — بِنْتٌ girl, أُمٌّ mother, شَمْسٌ sun — and are simply learned by heart. Stronger readers also met agreement: هَذِهِ بِنْتٌ نَشِيطَةٌ (“this is an active girl”).

Practise at home — five minutes a day

  1. “This or this?” walk. Point at things around the house; your child answers with a full sentence: هَذَا بَابٌ, هَذِهِ طَاوِلَةٌ.
  2. ة hunt. In any Arabic book or on a food label, ask your child to find and point to every ة.
  3. The magic shell. Write مُعَلِّم، طَالِب، قِطّ on cards; your child adds a paper “shell” with ة and reads the new word.
  4. One drawing, one sentence. Your child draws something and writes a sentence under it starting with هَذَا or هَذِهِ.
  5. Read the vowel marks aloud, slowly. Praise effort; never correct handwriting harshly.

Good to know: at home you may hear dialect forms (Egyptian دَه / دِي, Levantine هَاد / هَاي). That’s fine — at Nawras we learn the Fusha forms, the language of books and of the Qur’an.
