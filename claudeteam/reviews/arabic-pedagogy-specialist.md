# Review: arabic-pedagogy-specialist

**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Reviewer:** team reviewer 1 acting as `arabic-pedagogy-specialist` (read-only)
**Scope:** the هَذَا وَهَذِهِ lesson in all its forms: article, story, classroom deck (+ PLAN.md), book spec + web book + print PDF, mockup "word of the day", and guide.html's writing-pad section.

## Verdict: PASS with fixes (0 blocker · 3 major · 7 minor)

The sequence is sound in every form: input with pictures → a pair → the shell metaphor → the morph → the fence → agreement → writing → check. The classroom deck is the strongest piece. Its slides 3–17 restate the rule four ways (example, metaphor, body, chant), and slide 15 is a real TPR moment. The book opens with a greeting the child answers (p4), and its fence returns in exercises 5, 7 and 8. The writing station is complete in all three lessons.

The gaps are about **production** and **voice**. On screen the child never *says* anything. Every word a child taps is spoken by the device's synthetic voice. And the fence is taught as "learn by heart".

## Top 3 suggestions for what to build next
1. **«دَوْرُكَ!» echo moments** (APS-01) across the story, the article and the web book: the cheapest way to make the child *say* Fusha on screen.
2. **Sentence-builder game.** Under a picture, the child taps tiles into a line: [هَذَا | هَذِهِ] + [noun] + optional [صَغِيرٌ | صَغِيرَةٌ]. Noori reads the finished sentence back. A wrong tile gives a shake + a hint ("look, a shell at the end!"). Uses existing pictures and words, and follows recognition → guided production. A good fit for the article §5 and the web book's champions page.
3. **Digital "two baskets" sort.** The deck's variant made playable: picture cards fall into a هَذَا basket or a هَذِهِ basket, with بِنْتٌ / أُمٌّ / شَمْسٌ mixed in. Unlimited tries, no score. This brings the fence back in practice as well as in a quiz.
